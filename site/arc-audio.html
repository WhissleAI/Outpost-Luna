Our speech model

Arc Audio

Arc Transcriber Space is the on-device ear behind Outpost Luna. Its main benchmark is real NASA speech: Artemis crew audio and NASA podcasts, with astronauts, flight controllers and scientists talking about the Moon. Below: the data, how test sets stay clean, and the scores.

Why this corpus

General speech models stumble on exactly the words Outpost Luna needs most.

  • Real mission speech. Radio loops with noise, cross-talk and clipped phrasing. Clean read speech has none of this.
  • The Moon's vocabulary. Tycho, Shackleton, Sinus Medii, Orion, Artemis II. A general model writes "tie co" for Tycho.
  • Experts in plain language. NASA podcast guests explain the Moon the way learners ask about it.
  • Long, unscripted talk. Whole episodes, nobody reading a script: a hard, honest test.

How it is annotated

Every segment carries the labels the model returns at run time, ready for training the next model.

Spoken-form transcripts
Written as said: "apollo twelve", "nineteen sixty nine". The app's names lists use the same form, so heard and linked text line up.
Entity tags
Inline by type: lunar feature, site, celestial body, spacecraft, mission, crew, numbers, places, organisations.
Names lists
About 9 in 10 segments carry a names list like the app's, so a model learns to link each mention or say "not in the list".
Context
Earlier text and names from the same episode travel with each segment: context to use, not depend on.
Sounds and channel
Beeps, static, music beds and cross-talk are labelled, not dropped. Speakers stay anonymous.

The full rules for names lists are in the names-list guide.

Keeping test sets out

A score means something only if the model never saw the test audio.

  • Test recordings never enter. The 15 Artemis test recordings were skipped when the corpus was loaded.
  • Whole episodes, checked against every test set. Each episode is searched for every test sentence of 6+ words (Artemis, Apollo, read speech, space questions, held-out names, Common Voice); a segment equal to a 4+ word test sentence also counts. One hit removes the whole episode from the training split. 32 removed (20.3 h): 9 Artemis recordings that rebroadcast test audio, and 23 podcast episodes that matched only common phrases.
  • A new held-out test set. About 2.5% of each podcast series (at least 2 episodes), picked by a fixed hash. Only episodes with under 5% 8-word overlap with any earlier training text qualify. 19 episodes, 12.8 h, 10,410 segments (Houston We Have a Podcast 11, Small Steps, Giant Leaps 5, Curious Universe 3), frozen before any training on the corpus.
  • General speech as a guard. A fixed Common Voice English sample measures what space tuning costs everyday speech. Reported below.

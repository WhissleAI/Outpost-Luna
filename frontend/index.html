About

Outpost Luna: Junior Astronaut Mission Trainer

A voice game for kids 7 to 12. Artemis III hasn't landed yet: train at Flight School, then run Outpost Luna, a base at the Moon's south pole, where real NASA data decides what happens.

The challenge

Build a Junior Astronaut Mission Trainer

NASA Space Apps 2026 asks for a way for young people to practise what astronauts do: make decisions, manage resources and handle surprises on a mission.

How you play

Ten days on the Moon, one shift at a time

  • Pick a site. Each one trades sunlight, ice and how often you can see Earth.
  • Balance three things. Power, life support and radiation dose. Every choice costs something: shields use power that the greenhouse needs.
  • Talk to CAPCOM. "Shields to 40." "Send the rover for ice." "End shift." CAPCOM reads it back, and asks first before anything risky.
  • Call Houston. Radio takes about 1.3 seconds each way, so you wait for the answer, just like real crews.
  • Debrief. See your timeline, your grade, and what the past missions teach. Then try a different strategy.
  • Moon library: explore the Moon, the hardware people left there, and the Artemis II crew's own words.
How the Academy works

Train first, then command

Flight School is short lessons with CAPCOM: the Moon's south pole, radio talk, solar power, radiation and solar storms, air and ice, and lessons from Apollo. Each step takes a spoken, typed or tapped answer, and gives a hint after two misses. Every fact shows its NASA source. The last lesson is a check ride: two days on the real mission simulator with the real May 2024 solar storm. Pass it to earn the Mission Commander badge. Patches and your call sign are saved only on this device and never sent. The story is inspired by NASA's Artemis program: Artemis I flew uncrewed in 2022, Artemis II flew its crew around the Moon in April 2026, and Artemis III, the first landing near the south pole, has not happened yet. You train to help run Outpost Luna for the crews who come after. It uses NASA open data and is not endorsed by NASA.

How it works

Whissle's Arc models, running locally

Arc Tagger turns what a kid says into a mission command, and catches names and addresses so they are never looked up. Arc audio metadata hears when a kid starts and stops talking. Both run on a local model server, not in the cloud.

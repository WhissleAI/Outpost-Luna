Ask the Moon

A voice-first Moon explorer for kids. Say what you want to see, and the Moon turns to show you. Then listen to the Artemis II crew describe it in their own words.

The idea

Let kids explore the Moon by talking to it, and hear the astronauts who just flew past it

Most kids can't type "Mare Orientale," but they can say "show me the giant bullseye." Ask the Moon puts our Head Start voice-search pattern on a 3D Moon. A child speaks, we work out which place they mean, and the Moon turns to it.

The second part is new. In April 2026 the Artemis II crew flew around the Moon and narrated what they saw: colors, craters, Earthrise. NASA published 59 of those recordings. We transcribe and tag every one, then link each spoken place name to a point on the Moon. As a recording plays, the globe follows the astronauts' words.

"If you look at Orientale on the far side and then draw a line straight up to Ohm… relatively in the middle is an unnamed crater, and we would like to suggest it be called Integrity." (Artemis II crew)

The crew gave directions using other craters as landmarks. Following those directions is the kind of spatial-language task our tagger can learn, and it's the hook for the kids' missions.

How it works

Arc turns hours of crew audio into a Moon you can search by voice

INGESTNASA audio + imagery59 flyby clips, LRO color map, NASA image library, IAU crater names
ARC TRANSCRIBERWords + timing + feelWord timestamps, speaker, emotion (awe, laughter), sound events
ARC TAGGERWord-level tagsFEATURE, COLOR, GEO_TERM, DIRECTION, RELATION, AWE
INDEXMoon gazetteerEach tag links to a place (lat, lon) and a moment (clip, second)
VOICE SEARCHKid asks, Moon answersKid speech → intent + place → fly to it, highlight, play the crew's words

The same index answers questions in both directions. "Where is Aristarchus?" returns a place. "What did the astronauts say about Aristarchus?" returns the second in the clip where they said "yellow snow-capped mountain." "Where were they amazed?" filters on AWE tags.

In this build, place names and numbers come from Arc Tagger v0.3 (via the model server) linked to the gazetteer; colors, geology words and directions are still small rule lists until a lunar tagger domain exists. The two proposed-crater locations are estimates worked out from the crew's directions, not official coordinates.

More ideas

Eight things the same index makes possible

1. Voice Moon explorer

The core of the demo: say a place, or describe one ("the bullseye," "the yellow part"), and the Moon flies there with a kid-level fact.

ASR · intent · gazetteer
2. Crew log follow-along

Play an Artemis II recording while the globe follows the crew's words and the transcript highlights each one as it's spoken.

Arc Transcriber · Arc Tagger
3. Talk like an astronaut

The crew trained to describe terrain in geology words (they even practiced on a maple cookie). Kids describe a crater out loud and earn badges for using words like ejecta, rays and albedo.

Arc Tagger GEO_TERM scoring
4. Find-it missions

Clues written the way the crew gives directions: "northwest of Glushko, same latitude as Ohm." Kids tap the Moon to answer. Already in the prototype.

RELATION tags · spatial reasoning
5. Awe map

Pins where the crew gasped, laughed, or went quiet. Kids can tap a spot and hear how it felt to be there.

Arc emotion + AWE tags
6. SoundID: Mission Control quiz

Play a sound from the library (go/no-go poll, liftoff, splashdown, "Full send") and kids guess which part of the mission it's from.

Arc audio event tagging
7. Name a crater

The Artemis II crew proposed Integrity and Carroll. Kids pick an unnamed crater, say a name and why, and the class map keeps it.

ASR · shared class map
8. Show me pictures

"Show me Earthrise photos" searches NASA's image library through the same tags, so pictures and recordings both land on the globe.

Image captions → tagger index

Classroom mode builds on the Head Start work. A teacher asks a question and the class answers out loud. Multilingual ASR means a Spanish- or Hindi-speaking child can ask in their own language and still find "el Mar de la Tranquilidad."

What to train later

Models that make this work for real kids

  • Kid-speech ASR. Fine-tune on children's voices. Kids say "Oriental," "Aris-tar-cus" and "the Moon's eye," so we need fuzzy matching against the gazetteer.
  • Lunar Arc Tagger domain. Train on the crew transcripts plus synthetic kid questions. RELATION spans ("northwest of," "midpoint between") need their own head.
  • Spatial resolver. A small model that turns a RELATION parse into lat/lon. The two proposed craters make a ready-made test set.
  • Audio event tags for SoundID. Liftoff, countdown, comms beeps, splashdown, applause.
  • Safety for under-13s. Use Arc Tagger safety domains on what kids say. Store no audio by default.

Risks: proposed crater locations are estimates until the IAU confirms them. NASA media can't be used to imply endorsement. Voice input in the prototype depends on browser support.
